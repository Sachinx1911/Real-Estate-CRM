# Post-Sales Financial and Legal Process for Under-Construction Units in Maharashtra (incl. Navi Mumbai / CIDCO), for a Builder CRM

Research date: 24 October 2026. Scope: Maharashtra, with national rules (RERA Act, GST, Income-tax, RBI/NHB) where they apply. Each rate or rule gives its date or notification where one was found. "FLAG" marks items that may have changed or where sources conflict. Sources are a mix of primary documents (CBIC/GST Council FAQ, SBI rate page, RERA Act text mirrors, CBIC circulars, developer allotment letters and agreements uploaded for MahaRERA) and secondary commentary. Secondary-only items are marked as such.

## 1. Agreement for sale under the MahaRERA model: mandatory clauses, timelines, payment schedule caps, carpet area disclosure

### Takeaway
The promoter cannot take more than 10% of unit cost before a registered agreement for sale (RERA s.13). The agreement must follow the Maharashtra model (Annexure A). It carries cumulative stage-wise payment caps (10/30/45/70/75/80/85/95/balance at OC). MahaRERA Order No. 38 (Jan 2023) makes some clauses non-negotiable: carpet-area change with a 3% cap and a 45-day refund, termination after three defaults with notice, a 5-year defect liability, society formation and conveyance timelines, and force majeure. The MahaRERA model allotment letter (June 2022) adds a 2-month window to execute and register the agreement, plus tiered cancellation deductions capped at 2%.

### Cited Findings
**Statutory basis**
- RERA s.13(1): the promoter shall not accept more than 10% of the cost of the apartment/plot/building as advance or application fee without first executing a written agreement for sale and registering it. s.13(2) says the agreement must be in the prescribed form and specify development details, dates and manner of payments, the possession date, interest payable both ways on default, and other prescribed particulars. — [CourtBook: RERA s.13](https://courtbook.in/real-estate-regulation-and-development-act-2016/section-13)
- Rule 10 of the Maharashtra rules: the agreement must follow the Annexure 'A' model form. Earlier documents the allottee signed (application or allotment letters) do not limit the allottee's rights (secondary study guide). — [Open Exam Prep: Stamp duty, registration & AFS](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/in-maharera-agent-coc/transaction-due-diligence-land-records/stamp-duty-registration-agreements)
- MahaREAT held it illegal to demand further payment from homebuyers after taking over 20% of the flat price without executing an agreement for sale. The related MahaRERA order told the builder to finalise the agreement within one month. — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/consumer-cases/maha-reat-additional-payment-homebuyers-executing-agreement-sale-illegal-254580)

**Model payment schedule (Annexure A, clause 1(c)): cumulative ceilings on total consideration**
- Not exceeding 10% as advance/application fee before agreement execution. Not exceeding 30% after execution of the agreement. 45% on completion of plinth. 70% on completion of slabs, including podiums and stilts. 75% on completion of walls, internal plaster, floorings, doors and windows of the apartment. 80% on completion of sanitary fittings, staircases, lift wells and lobbies up to the floor of the apartment. 85% on completion of external plumbing, external plaster, elevation and terraces with waterproofing. 95% on completion of lifts, water pumps, electrical fittings, electro-mechanical and environment requirements, entrance lobby, plinth protection and paving. Balance at handover of possession, on or after receipt of OC or completion certificate (secondary study guide; the official form was not retrieved). — [Open Exam Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/in-maharera-agent-coc/transaction-due-diligence-land-records/stamp-duty-registration-agreements)
- A developer draft agreement uploaded to MahaRERA (dated 15 September 2017, Mumbai project) uses the same milestones with non-cumulative tranches: 10% at booking (50% of earnest money), 20% on agreement execution, 15% on plinth, 25% across slabs (proportionate per slab), 5% walls/plaster/flooring/doors/windows, 5% staircases/lift wells/lobbies, 5% external plumbing/plaster/elevation/waterproofing, 10% lifts/pumps/electrical/lobby/paving, and the balance at possession on or after OC/CC. — [Developer draft AFS uploaded for MahaRERA (housiey resource)](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1059_1766741592382-482534564.pdf)
- A Thane-district developer's allotment-letter payment plan (Annexure A) shows how real schedules are split into many sub-stages. Booking token 1%, 4% within 7 days and 5% within 15 days of booking (10% in total), 15% on excavation, then plinth, podium slabs, floor-wise slab "initiation" tranches of 3–4% each, then 5% tranches for blockwork, internal plaster/flooring/tiling, and external plumbing/electrical/lift/doors, with the last 5% on possession/OC. — [Developer allotment letter, Thane (housiey resource)](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1359_1766816111271-620221494.pdf)
- A Pune forum example shows a developer's construction-linked plan front-loading 20% at plinth, followed by 2% per slab. — [ValuePickr forum](https://forum.valuepickr.com/t/raymond-realty-ltd-high-roce-demerger-play/205128?page=11)
- Pre-RERA agreements: a buyer argued that RERA's ~70% slab cap should override a pre-RERA agreement that required ~99.5% by the final slab. The builder relied on the agreement. This is an opinion Q&A, not a ruling. — [Kaanoon](https://www.kaanoon.com/230507/maharera-applicability-of-rera-payment-plan-pre-rera-registration-and-post-rera-demand)

**MahaRERA model allotment letter and non-negotiable AFS clauses**
- MahaRERA issued a model allotment letter and agreement for sale in 2022. The allotment letter prescribes a minimum period for the allottee to cancel and caps the promoter's deduction at a maximum of 2%. — [AZB & Partners (30 Jun 2022)](https://www.azbpartners.com/bank/maharera-model-allotment-letter-and-agreement-for-sale/); [Argus Partners](https://www.argus-p.com/updates/updates/maharera-proforma-of-allotment-letter-and-agreement-for-sale/); the WIRC-ICAI document is titled "Order 30 dt 03.06.2022" — [WIRC-ICAI](https://old.wirc-icai.org/images/material/Order-30dt-03-06-2022-AshwinShah.pdf)
- Model allotment letter terms, as used by a developer in 2025–26:
  - The allottee must execute the AFS and appear for registration before the Sub-Registrar within 2 months of the allotment letter (extendable by mutual understanding).
  - If not, the promoter serves a 15-day notice, may then cancel, forfeit up to 2% of unit cost, and refund the balance without interest within 45 days of the notice expiring. If the refund is late, interest at SBI highest MCLR + 2% applies.
  - Unpaid booking instalments: a 15-day notice, then cancellation with 2% forfeiture and a refund within 45 days.
  - Car parking is allotted "free of any consideration".
  - Interest on delayed payment is SBI highest MCLR + 2%.
  - The allottee cannot transfer or assign the letter without the promoter's prior written consent.
  - — [Developer allotment letter (housiey resource)](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1359_1766816111271-620221494.pdf)
- MahaRERA Order No. 38 (update released 12 Jan 2023, effective immediately) made these AFS clauses non-negotiable:
  - force majeure, limited to natural calamities;
  - formation of the association within 3 months of a majority of allottees booking (absent local law);
  - conveyance within 3 months of OC (absent local law);
  - defect liability of 5 years;
  - carpet-area reduction, with the excess refunded within 45 days with interest from the date of payment;
  - termination for allottee default only after three payment defaults plus written notice and an email, with a refund within 30 days of termination.
  - The earlier order of 3 June 2022 requires any deviation from Annexure A to be highlighted in a different colour. Registration applications that violate these clauses are rejected.
  - — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/real-estate/1308918/mahrera-update-|-non-negotiable-clauses-in-the-agreement-for-sale)
- Area-difference, termination and parking clauses cannot be modified. Force majeure, association formation, conveyance and defect-liability wording can change but must be shown in a deviation report (search-result summary of law-firm commentary). — [SNR Law](https://www.snrlaw.in/agreement-for-sale-under-the-real-estate-regulation-and-development-act-2016-format-mandatory-clauses-and-legal-implications/)

**Carpet area**
- Model clause (as in the developer draft, cl. 3.7): after OC the promoter confirms the final carpet area, subject to a 3% variation cap, and the price is recalculated. If the area shrinks within 3%, the excess is refunded within 45 days with annual interest at the RERA Rules rate from the date the excess was paid. If it grows, the promoter demands the additional amount. — [Developer draft AFS](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1059_1766741592382-482534564.pdf)
- A Free Press Journal column rejects the developer view that a shortfall within 3% needs no refund. Any reduction within the cap must be refunded with interest. — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/mumbai/consumer-connect-maharera-mandates-refund-for-carpet-area-shortfall-within-3-variation-cap)
- The allotment letter states unit size as "RERA Carpet area [] sq. meters equivalent to [] sq. feet" and the price "exclusive of GST, stamp duty and registration charges". — [Developer allotment letter](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1359_1766816111271-620221494.pdf)

**Other AFS clauses relevant to the CRM (developer draft, 2017)**
- cl. 3.8 / 5.1.9: the promoter may appropriate payments against any outstanding head, including unpaid TDS or missing TDS certificates.
- cl. 3.9: payment is due within 7 days of a written demand.
- cl. 5.1.4: a milestone intimation, with proof of dispatch (including email), is sufficient proof that the milestone was reached.
- cl. 5.1.6: the promoter has a first charge on the unit for dues.
- cl. 5.1.2: payment is by account-payee cheque/DD/RTGS/NEFT to the account named in the schedule.
- cl. 13: nomination of a nominee.
- cl. 19: after executing the AFS the promoter shall not mortgage or create a charge on the unit.
- — [Developer draft AFS](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1059_1766741592382-482534564.pdf)

### Inferences
- The CRM needs a payment-plan validator. For every plan template, cumulative % at each milestone must be at or below the model cap (10% pre-AFS, 30% post-AFS, 45% plinth, 70% slabs, and so on). The Thane example reaches 25% by excavation and 35% by plinth, within the caps.
- The CRM needs date fields and SLA timers for: allotment letter date, AFS execution deadline (+2 months), 15-day default notice, cancellation date, refund-due date (+45 days for the allotment stage, +30 days for termination after AFS), and carpet-area true-up on OC with a refund-due date (+45 days).
- Store RERA carpet area (sq m and sq ft), exclusive-area/balcony fields, and final post-OC carpet area. Auto-compute the price delta and flag variance above 3% (that needs an allottee decision).
- Any deviation from the model AFS template should be versioned and highlighted (Order of 3 June 2022).

### Gaps
- The official MahaRERA Annexure A text (current version) and the Order No. 30/38 PDFs were not retrieved directly. The stage caps come from a secondary study guide, corroborated by developer documents.
- No 2025–2026 MahaRERA changes to the model AFS or allotment letter were found. A 2026 roundup mentions circulars on "execution of documents and recovery of amounts awarded" but gave no details ([Chambers / S&R roundup](https://chambers.com/articles/regulatory-updates-rera-october-2025-to-march-2026)). FLAG: check the MahaRERA circulars page.

## 2. Construction-linked plans, demand letters, architect certificates, interest on delay (both directions), grace periods

### Takeaway
Rule 18 (Maharashtra RERA Rules 2017) fixes interest both ways at SBI's highest MCLR + 2%, as simple interest. From 15 October 2026 SBI's highest MCLR (3-year) is 8.95%, so the RERA rate is 10.95% p.a. Buyer-default interest runs from due date to payment. Promoter-delay interest runs monthly from the agreed possession date until possession is handed over, or the buyer can exit with a refund plus interest from each payment date. Withdrawals from the RERA account need architect (Form 1), engineer (Form 2) and CA (Form 3) certificates.

### Cited Findings
- Interest rate wording in MahaRERA allotment letters and AFS: "State Bank of India highest Marginal Cost of Lending Rate plus two percent". The AFS default clause applies it from the due date until full payment. — [Developer allotment letter](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1359_1766816111271-620221494.pdf); [Developer draft AFS cl. 5.1.7](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1059_1766741592382-482534564.pdf)
- SBI MCLR, revised effective **15 October 2026**: Overnight 7.85%, 1M 7.85%, 3M 8.40% (from 8.25%), 6M 8.75% (from 8.60%), 1Y 8.85% (from 8.70%), 2Y 8.90% (from 8.75%), 3Y 8.95% (from 8.80%). — [SBI MCLR page](https://sbi.bank.in/web/interest-rates/interest-rates/mclr)
- Secondary guide: Rule 18 = SBI highest MCLR + 2% p.a., the same rate both ways, simple interest unless the Tribunal orders otherwise. Formula I = P × R/100 × M/12 (M = D/365×12 for days). Worked example: ₹60 lakh at 10.50% (MCLR 8.50%) for 18 months of delay = ₹52,500/month, ₹9.45 lakh in total. In an exit refund, interest runs on each payment from its date to the refund date. FLAG: the same page also says "1-Year MCLR", which conflicts with "highest". — [Open Exam Prep: delay interest](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/in-maharera-agent-coc/allottee-rights-duties-delays/delay-interest-compensation-math)
- RERA s.18(1): if the promoter fails to give possession on the agreed date, the allottee may withdraw and get a refund with interest and compensation. If the allottee stays, the promoter pays "interest for every month of delay, till the handing over of the possession". — [CourtBook: RERA s.18](https://courtbook.in/real-estate-regulation-and-development-act-2016/section-18)
- RERA s.19(6)–(8): the allottee must pay on time as per the agreement, plus a share of registration charges, municipal taxes, water and electricity, maintenance, ground rent and so on. Interest at the prescribed rate applies on delay. Both can be reduced by mutual agreement. — [CourtBook: RERA s.19](https://courtbook.in/real-estate-regulation-and-development-act-2016/section-19)
- Model-type AFS: on promoter delay (other than force majeure, government orders, court stays), the allottee may (a) serve an "Interest Notice" and receive interest at SBI highest MCLR + 2% for every month of delay on the price paid, until possession is offered; or (b) terminate, with a refund within 30 days plus interest from each receipt date. Choosing (a) bars (b) later. — [Developer draft AFS cl. 7.1.3–7.1.4](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1059_1766741592382-482534564.pdf)
- Bombay High Court upheld MahaREAT: delay interest at SBI MCLR + 2% runs until actual physical possession is handed over with OC conditions complied. The builder's argument that liability ended two months after OC was rejected. — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/consumer-cases/bombay-high-court-upholds-maharashtra-real-estate-appellate-tribunals-decision-to-impose-interest-on-keyana-estate-for-delayed-possession-307568)
- Grace period: the developer allotment letter sets the possession date as "on or before [date], along with an extension of 12 (twelve) months", plus a "reasonable extension" for force majeure. Order No. 38 limits force majeure to natural calamities affecting the project. — [Developer allotment letter](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1359_1766816111271-620221494.pdf); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/real-estate/1308918/mahrera-update-|-non-negotiable-clauses-in-the-agreement-for-sale)
- Default/termination: after three defaults in instalment payment, the promoter may terminate after a 15-day written default notice (courier/email/RPAD) setting out the breach. — [Developer draft AFS cl. 5.1.8](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1059_1766741592382-482534564.pdf)
- Certificates: RERA s.4 requires withdrawals from the separate account to be in proportion to completion, certified by an engineer, an architect and a practising CA. MahaRERA Circular 39/2021 (28 Dec 2021) requires Forms 1 (architect: % work completed per building/wing), 2 (engineer: cost incurred and % completion) and 3 (CA: project cost, deposits, withdrawals) at every withdrawal, submitted to the bank and uploaded to MahaRERA. The CA certifying withdrawals must not be the statutory auditor. — [WIRC-ICAI Form 3 material](https://old.wirc-icai.org/images/material/CH-28-CA-certificate-form3.pdf); [WIRC-ICAI MahaRERA Feb 2022 update](https://old.wirc-icai.org/images/law-updates/Maha-Rera-Feb-2022.pdf)
- A January 2022 circular made Forms 1, 2, 3, 5 and 2A publicly viewable, so buyers can check them. — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-maharera-ushers-in-era-of-transparency)

### Inferences
- Current RERA interest rate: 8.95% (SBI 3-year MCLR, the highest tenor, effective 15 Oct 2026) + 2% = **10.95% p.a. simple**. The rate before 15 Oct 2026 was 8.80% + 2% = 10.80%. The CRM should hold an MCLR rate table with effective dates and support both "rate on date of default" and "floating" methods, because no source settled which applies.
- A demand-letter engine should: trigger on milestone completion (architect certificate or site-engineer sign-off uploaded), generate a demand with base instalment, GST, and TDS guidance; set the due date (7 or 15 days per agreement); send by email/courier with dispatch proof logged (used as legal proof of service); calculate interest daily from the due date; and escalate to a 15-day default notice, with a termination workflow after the third default.
- For promoter delay, the CRM needs a delay-interest ledger per unit: payable monthly from the agreed or extended possession date to the date possession is offered or handed over, on the amount paid to date. The ledger should support adjustment against dues, and the exit/refund calculation (each receipt × rate × days/365).

### Gaps
- The Rule 18 gazette text was not retrieved directly. In particular, it is unclear whether "highest MCLR" means the highest tenor on the date of each default or a fixed rate. Interpretation should be confirmed with counsel.
- No source gave a statutory "grace period" for buyer payments. Grace periods are contractual.

## 3. GST on under-construction property

### Takeaway
Since 1 April 2019 (Notification 03/2019-CT(Rate) amending 11/2017-CT(Rate)), residential units are 1% (affordable) or 5% (others) without ITC, on total consideration after a deemed one-third land deduction. Commercial units in a residential real estate project (commercial carpet area 15% or less) are 5% without ITC. Commercial units in other projects are an effective 12% with ITC. GST 2.0 (effective 22 September 2025) left the residential rates unchanged. FLAG: sources conflict on whether commercial moved to 18% effective. Preferential location charges (PLC) take the construction rate (circular dated 11 Oct 2024). There is no GST after the completion certificate or first occupation.

### Cited Findings
- Rates from 1 April 2019, "after deduction of value of land", on total consideration:
  - affordable residential: 1% without ITC;
  - other residential: 5% without ITC;
  - commercial apartments (shops, offices, godowns) in a Residential Real Estate Project (RREP): 5% without ITC;
  - commercial apartments in an REP other than an RREP: 12% with ITC.
  - An RREP is a project where commercial carpet area is no more than 15% of total carpet area.
  - Ongoing projects could opt for the old 8%/12% with ITC by 10 May 2019; projects starting on or after 1 April 2019 have no option.
  - — [GST Council/TRU FAQ on real estate, F.No.354/32/2019-TRU, 7 May 2019](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-02/faq-real-estate-sector.pdf)
- Affordable residential apartment: carpet area up to 60 sq m in metropolitan cities or 90 sq m elsewhere, and a gross amount charged of no more than ₹45 lakh. "Mumbai (whole of MMR)" is a metropolitan city. — [GST FAQ Q2–Q3](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-02/faq-real-estate-sector.pdf)
- One-third land abatement is mandatory. Actual land value cannot be used instead (para 2 of Notification 11/2017-CTR). — [GST FAQ Q36](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-02/faq-real-estate-sector.pdf)
- Conditions for the 1%/5% rates:
  - at least 80% of inputs and input services (excluding TDR, FSI, long-term lease, electricity, diesel, petrol and natural gas) must be bought from registered suppliers;
  - the shortfall is taxed at 18% under RCM, except cement, which is taxed under RCM at the applicable rate (28% in 2019);
  - tax is paid in cash with no ITC, with project-wise accounts and ITC-not-availed reporting in GSTR-3B.
  - — [GST FAQ Q8–Q10](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-02/faq-real-estate-sector.pdf)
- After GST 2.0, cement went from 28% to 18%. — [Ujjivan SFB blog](https://www.ujjivansfb.bank.in/banking-blogs/personal-finance/gst-rate-rationalisation-real-estate-impact)
- TDR/FSI/long-term lease for residential units booked before the CC or first occupation are exempt. The portion relating to units unbooked at CC attracts 18% under RCM, capped at 1% or 5% of the unit value; for commercial it is 18%. The liability arises on the CC or first occupation date. — [GST FAQ Q11–Q15](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-02/faq-real-estate-sector.pdf)
- Cancellation: a credit note under s.34 is allowed for a price change or cancellation, and tax is adjusted if the amount is refunded to the buyer. If a unit was booked at the old 8%/12% with ITC and is then cancelled and rebooked at the new rates (or sold after CC), the ITC must be reversed. — [GST FAQ Q22](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-02/faq-real-estate-sector.pdf)
- No GST after completion: sales after completion or first occupation are outside GST (Schedule III/II para 5(b)). "First occupation" follows law; a part OC does not count as first occupation of the whole project. — [GST FAQ Q29](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-02/faq-real-estate-sector.pdf); [Busy.in summary](https://busy.in/gst/applicability-of-gst-on-real-estate.md)
- Item (if) of Heading 9954 (construction of commercial apartments in a non-residential REP) is listed at 9% CGST + 9% SGST (18%). With the one-third land deduction, that gives the "12% on total consideration". — [GSTGyaan: Heading 9954 rates](https://gstgyaan.com/cgst-rate-on-construction-services-Heading-9954)
- GST 2.0: the 56th GST Council meeting moved to 5%/18% slabs, effective 22 September 2025. — [Housing.com](https://housing.com/news/gst-on-rent)
- Notification 15/2025-CT(Rate) dated 17 Sep 2025 amended Notification 11/2017 for services to implement the 56th Council recommendations. — [ICAI-IDTC listing](https://idtc.icai.org/gst_new.php?gst_type=CENTRAL+GOODS+AND+SERVICES+TAX)
- Residential stayed at 1%/5% after GST 2.0. — [Housiey 2026 explainer](https://housiey.com/blogs/?p=7543); [Kanakkupillai](https://www.kanakkupillai.com/learn/gst-on-residential-property/)
- FLAG on commercial: some sources say commercial under-construction moved to 18% with ITC from 22 Sep 2025; others still list 12% with ITC for non-RREP commercial. — [Busy.in builders page](https://busy.in/gst-rates/builders/); [Legal Suvidha 2026](https://legalsuvidha.com/blog/gst-on-real-estate-industry)
- Works contracts from a contractor to a promoter: 12% (affordable) or 18% (other residential); commercial 18%. The 15/2025 amendment reportedly moved some 12% construction entries to 18%. — [GST FAQ Q32](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-02/faq-real-estate-sector.pdf); [GSTGyaan](https://gstgyaan.com/cgst-rate-on-construction-services-Heading-9954)
- PLC: the 54th GST Council meeting (9 Sep 2024) recommended that PLC not be taxed separately. A clarification dated 11 Oct 2024 (issued under s.168(1) CGST) treats PLC charged before the CC as part of composite construction service at the same rate. The Punjab & Haryana HC (DLF Ltd v. CCGST, CWP 17530/2022, decided 13 May 2026) held the circular binding and retrospective. — [Taxo.online](https://taxo.online/latest-news/preferential-location-charges-plc-is-taxable-at-the-same-gst-rate-applicable-to-construction-services-and-cannot-be-treated-as-an-independent-supply-punjab-and-haryana-high-court/); [TaxHeal](https://www.taxheal.com/?p=132072); [Piramal Realty blog](https://www.piramalrealty.com/blogs/how-gst-applies-when-you-buy-an-under-construction-flat)
- Parking and clubhouse/amenity fees bundled into the single price of an under-construction flat take the flat's rate. Parking billed as a separate, optional right to use has been taxed at 18% in some rulings (secondary). — [PropNewz 2026](https://www.propnewz.com/blog/bengaluru-gst-parking-clubhouse-amenity-charges-flat-buyer-2026-09-17)
- Maharashtra developer AFS schedules bill these separately "with taxes to be paid separately at applicable rates": society formation charges, utility deposits, a one-time club membership fee, legal costs, development charges, share money, corpus, and the deposit towards provisional monthly outgoings. The car park is stated as not charged. — [Developer draft AFS, 8th/9th Schedules and cl. 3.11](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1059_1766741592382-482534564.pdf)
- Maharashtra AAAR (Puranik Builders, Order 19/2022-23): "other charges" collected from customers are taxable as independent services under their own SAC codes. The builder was charging 18%. — [MH AAAR order](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-02/mh_aaar_order_no._19-2022-23_of_puranik_builders_ltd.pdf)
- Maintenance by RWAs is exempt up to ₹7,500 per month per member, and the RWA need not register if turnover is ₹20 lakh or less. Above ₹7,500 the whole amount is taxed, and the ceiling applies separately to each apartment. — [CBIC Circular 109/28/2019-GST, 22 Jul 2019](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/pdf/circular-cgst-109n.pdf)
- When the builder runs maintenance before the RWA forms, GST "applies differently and often more stringently" (not detailed). — [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/gst-on-apartment-maintenance-what-residents-need-to-know-when-the-builder-is-in-charge)

### Inferences
- Rate engine per unit: classify the project (RREP or not; ongoing-old-rate or new), then the unit (affordable test: carpet ≤60 sq m in MMR including Navi Mumbai, and gross amount ≤₹45 lakh; residential or commercial). Apply an effective rate on gross consideration, split CGST/SGST 50:50 (for example 5% = 2.5% + 2.5%). The nominal rate equivalents follow arithmetically from the one-third land deduction: 1% effective = 1.5% on 2/3, 5% = 7.5% on 2/3, 12% = 18% on 2/3.
- Treat as composite with the unit price (same rate): PLC, floor-rise and bundled amenities. Treat as separate supplies (normally 18%) and flag for tax review: club membership billed separately, legal charges, society formation charges, maintenance collected by the builder, transfer fees, and interest on delayed payment (interest is generally treated as part of the value of supply; not verified here).
- Exclude from GST: stamp duty, registration fees, share money paid to the society, and corpus/sinking funds held in trust. Treatment varies; get a CA sign-off per head.
- Invoice per demand: generate a tax invoice (or receipt voucher for advances) for every instalment demand or receipt, with project-wise GST registration, HSN/SAC 9954 and a land-abatement note. After the OC/CC date, block GST on instalments for that tower or wing and alert finance.
- If GST 2.0 changed effective commercial rates (FLAG), the CRM needs rate tables with effective dates and transition logic for invoices around 22 Sep 2025.

### Gaps
- The CBIC text of Notification 15/2025-CT(Rate) was not retrieved. Whether item (if) (commercial, non-RREP) and the 1%/5% entries were renumbered or re-rated is unverified.
- The circular number for the 11 Oct 2024 PLC clarification is not confirmed in the retrieved sources (commonly cited as Circular 240/34/2024-GST; FLAG, verify).
- No authoritative source on GST for builder-collected maintenance before society handover, legal charges, or transfer fees in Maharashtra beyond the Puranik AAAR.

## 4. TDS on property purchase (s.194-IA, now s.393 of the Income-tax Act 2025): 26QB/16B, reconciliation, NRI cases

### Takeaway
The buyer deducts 1% on each instalment when total consideration or stamp-duty value is ₹50 lakh or more. Consideration includes parking, club, maintenance and similar fees. The tax is deposited within 30 days from the end of the month of deduction (Form 26QB up to 31 March 2026). From 1 April 2026 the Income-tax Act 2025 moved this to s.393(1), and secondary sources say Form 141 replaces 26QB. FLAG: the official Income Tax Department chart still cites 194-IA/26QB. The rate is 20% if the seller's PAN is inoperative. Resale by an NRI seller falls under old s.195, now s.393(2) Sl.17.

### Cited Findings
- 194-IA: "Any person, being a transferee, responsible for paying" consideration to a resident transferor for immovable property (other than agricultural land) deducts 1%. It applies when sale consideration or stamp-duty value is ₹50 lakh or more, using the higher of the two. Deposit with Form 26QB within 30 days of month-end (Income Tax Dept chart for AY 2026-27). — [Income Tax Dept chart](https://incometaxindia.gov.in/Charts%20%20Tables/TDS%20Purchase%20of%20Immovable%20property.htm); [Income Tax Dept page](https://incometaxindia.gov.in/w/tds-from-sum-paid-to-buy-an-immovable-property)
- TDS is due on each instalment and on the whole amount, not only the excess over ₹50 lakh. Consideration includes club membership, car parking, electricity/water facility fees, maintenance and advance fees. The rate is 20% if PAN is not provided. — [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/how-to-file-tds-on-sale-property)
- The developer AFS requires the allottee to deduct TDS from each instalment and furnish the TDS certificate. Any loss of credit to the promoter is recovered from the allottee, and payments can be appropriated against TDS shortfalls. — [Developer draft AFS cl. 3.12, 5.1.9](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1059_1766741592382-482534564.pdf)
- Multiple buyers/sellers: each buyer remits their share and files a separate 26QB per buyer–seller pair, even if each share is below ₹50 lakh. The 20% rate applies where the transferor's PAN is not linked to Aadhaar. Interest is 1.5% per month for late deposit. The s.234E late fee is ₹200/day, capped at the TDS amount. Penalty falls under s.271H (old Act). — [TaxGuru: TDS on property under IT Act 2025](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/tds-immovable-property-purchases-compliance-income-tax-act-2025.html)
- Income-tax Act 2025 (from 1 April 2026):
  - property TDS is consolidated in s.393(1) [Table Sl. No. 3(i)], replacing 194-IA, at 1% of the higher of consideration or stamp-duty value — [StudyCafe](https://studycafe.in/section-3931-explained-new-tds-rules-on-property-purchase-416842.html);
  - Form 26QB is replaced by Form 141 — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/tds-immovable-property-purchases-compliance-income-tax-act-2025.html); [TheTaxCorp](https://thetaxcorp.in/article/tds-on-property-transactions-how-section-393-of-the-income-tax-act-2025);
  - an insurer article maps 194-IA to "Section 123" — [ABSLI](https://lifeinsurance.adityabirlacapital.com/articles/income-tax/form-26qb/). FLAG: conflicting and unverified.
  - Transition: if payment or credit was on or before 31 March 2026, the 1961 Act and Form 26QB apply (secondary search summary).
- Non-resident seller (old s.195): now s.393(2) Table Sl. No. 17. Long-term capital asset rate is 12.5% plus surcharge plus 4% cess. Form 27Q is replaced by Form 144. The quarterly Form 144/TAN route applies to TDS up to 30 Sep 2026; from 1 Oct 2026 TAN and quarterly returns are dispensed with (PAN-based). — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/income-tax/tds-immovable-property-purchases-compliance-income-tax-act-2025.html)
- 194-IA requires the seller to be resident (s.195 for NRI sellers). ClearTax also notes that under Budget 2026 non-resident buyers or sellers can use a PAN-based facility without a TAN. — [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/how-to-file-tds-on-sale-property)
- Form 16B is available on TRACES about 10–15 days after deposit (secondary). — [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/how-to-file-tds-on-sale-property)

### Inferences
- Builder sales: the builder is the resident transferor, so every buyer, including NRI buyers, deducts 1% under 194-IA/393(1). Section 195 matters only when the CRM handles resale or nomination by an NRI first allottee.
- Receipt design: each instalment receipt should record gross demand, amount received by bank transfer, and TDS deducted (expected 1% of the base, with a flag where GST is included or excluded per policy). It should also hold the buyer's challan/acknowledgement number (26QB or Form 141), date, PAN, and Form 16B received (Y/N). Treat TDS as a deemed receipt only after it appears in the builder's Form 26AS/AIS. Run a periodic 26AS-vs-CRM reconciliation, chase missing 16Bs, and post the shortfall to the customer ledger (the AFS allows recovery).
- Joint buyers: TDS is split by ownership share. Each co-buyer needs their own challan per seller. Since 1 Oct 2024 (Finance (No.2) Act 2024), the ₹50 lakh test is on aggregate consideration. That date comes from background knowledge, not a retrieved source, so verify it.
- Whether TDS is calculated on the GST component is not settled in the retrieved sources. CBDT Circular 23/2017 (exclusion of GST) is commonly read as limited to rent (194-I). The CRM should make this configurable.

### Gaps
- The official Income-tax Rules 2026 form numbers (Form 141 vs 26QB; the replacement for Form 16B) and the Form 16B issuance deadline under the new rules were not confirmed from an official source.
- Section 201(1A) interest rates (1%/1.5%) under the new Act were not confirmed.

## 5. Stamp duty and registration in Maharashtra

### Takeaway
Stamp duty is charged on the higher of agreement value and Ready Reckoner (ASR) value. In Mumbai it is 6% (5% + 1% metro cess) and 5% for women. In Pune, Thane, Nagpur and PCMC it is 7% (5% + 1% metro cess + 1% LBT/surcharge) and 6% for women. FLAG: for Navi Mumbai, sources say 7%, with some arguing 6%. Registration fee is 1%, capped at ₹30,000. ASR rates were frozen for FY 2026-27 (from 1 April 2026). The women's 1% concession dates from 31 March 2021; its 15-year resale bar was deleted on 26 May 2023 (sources differ). Documents must be presented within 4 months of execution. The Sub-Registrar issues Index-II after registration.

### Cited Findings
- Rates by area:
  - Mumbai (BMC): 5% base + 1% metro cess, no LBT, giving 6% for men and 5% for women.
  - Pune/PCMC/Thane/Nagpur: 5% + 1% metro cess + 1% LBT, giving 7% for men and 6% for women.
  - Duty is charged on the higher of consideration or ASR, which is published each 1 April.

- Conflicting: Mumbai 7% (5% + 1% LBT + 1% metro cess). — [Richify](https://www.richify.ai/in/hi/stamp-duty-maharashtra)
- Navi Mumbai: 7% for a sole male buyer (5% + 1% metro cess + 1% LBT) and 6% for a sole female buyer. Joint male+female is 7%; two women, 6%. Some 2026 sources argue LBT does not apply in NMMC (giving 6%), and treatment may differ for CIDCO/Panvel/Raigad-side nodes. GRAS district selection: Raigad for Kharghar/Panvel/Ulwe, Thane for Vashi/Nerul/Belapur. — [Square Yards (updated 20 Aug 2026)](https://www.squareyards.com/blog/stamp-duty-and-registration-charges-in-navi-mumbai); [HomeFirst](https://homefirstindia.com/blog/article/stamp-duty-registration-charges-maharashtra)

## 6. Home loans: tripartite agreement, NOC/permission to mortgage, stage-wise disbursement, APF, subvention

### Takeaway
RBI (3 Sept 2013) requires banks to disburse individual housing loans in step with construction stages and bars upfront disbursal for under-construction projects (the 80:20 and 75:25 schemes). NHB (20 Nov 2025) issued a similar advisory to HFCs on subvention schemes. Model AFS: the allottee may mortgage the unit only with the promoter's prior written consent (refused if in default), and lenders must pay into the promoter's designated account.

### Cited Findings
- RBI/2013-14/217 DBOD.BP.BC.No.51/08.12.015/2013-14 (3 Sep 2013): disbursal "should be closely linked to the stages of construction"; no upfront disbursal for incomplete or under-construction projects; the 80:20/75:25 schemes were the concern. A later circular allows disbursal per payment schedules of government or statutory housing bodies with no history of non-completion. — [RBI circular PDF](https://website.rbi.org.in/documents/87730/39710850/HHSI11092013.pdf); [RBI circular index](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_CircularIndexDisplay.aspx?Id=8429)
- NHB advisory dated 20 November 2025, issued at the Department of Financial Services' request after a Karnataka High Court order on subvention schemes: HFCs must keep disbursals strictly linked to construction stages, with no upfront disbursal. — [NHB advisory PDF](https://www.nhb.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/15.-Disbursement-of-Housing-Loan-to-individuals-linked-to-the-stages-of-construction_November-20-2025.pdf)
- Courts have heard homebuyer petitions alleging banks disbursed to developers despite these circulars. — [S.S. Rana](https://ssrana.in/articles/homebuyer-not-to-be-penalised-for-defaults-of-builder/)
- AFS loan clause:
  - The allottee may avail a loan and mortgage the unit with the promoter's prior written consent, which may be refused if the allottee is in default.
  - All loan costs are the allottee's; loan documents cannot bind the promoter.
  - The lender must disburse amounts due to the promoter in the agreed manner, to the promoter's account.
  - The promoter may securitise receivables and redirect payments to a financier on intimation (cl. 3.10).
  - The promoter shall not mortgage the sold unit after the AFS (cl. 19).
  - — [Developer draft AFS cl. 5.1.3, 5.2, 3.10, 19](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1059_1766741592382-482534564.pdf)
- On CIDCO-leased plots, some banks require a CIDCO NOC "for creation of mortgage" before disbursement. Whether it is needed depends on the lease deed (forum opinions, not official). — [Kaanoon](https://www.kaanoon.com/91295/noc-from-cidco-for-mortgage)

### Inferences
- Loan-tracking module per booking: lender, sanction amount and date, loan account number, own-contribution vs loan split per demand, "permission to mortgage / NOC" letter (generated after verifying no dues), tripartite agreement status (where the lender requires one), APF status of the project with each lender, and a disbursement request letter per demand (with architect certificate or stage completion letter, demand letter, and own-contribution receipt). Track disbursement received vs demanded and route shortfalls back to the customer.
- Subvention/pre-EMI schemes need special handling: track any builder-borne pre-EMI interest liability and expiry date. The NHB 2025 advisory makes non-stage-linked disbursal non-compliant, so the CRM should block plan templates that imply upfront loan disbursal.

### Gaps
- No primary source was found on the standard Maharashtra tripartite agreement format, APF documentation, or a 2025–2026 RBI master direction update on subvention.

## 8. Possession: OC/CC, possession letter, handover checklist, defect liability, snag lists

### Takeaway
After the OC, the promoter gives written notice offering possession (model-type AFS: within 7 days of OC). The allottee must take possession within 15 days of the date in the notice (contract) and within 2 months of OC (RERA s.19(10)). Outgoings and maintenance start 15 days after OC whether or not possession is taken. The final instalment, deposits and charges must be paid before handover. Defect liability is 5 years from possession: the promoter rectifies free of cost within 30 days or pays compensation.

### Cited Findings
- RERA s.19(10): every allottee shall take physical possession "within a period of two months of the occupancy certificate". s.19(11): the allottee participates in the conveyance deed. — [CourtBook: s.19](https://courtbook.in/real-estate-regulation-and-development-act-2016/section-19)
- RERA s.14(3): defects (structural, workmanship, quality, services, other obligations) reported within 5 years of handover must be rectified "without further charge, within thirty days", or the allottee may claim compensation. — [CourtBook: s.14](https://courtbook.in/real-estate-regulation-and-development-act-2016/section-14)
- Model-type AFS sequence:
  - cl. 7.1.6: a possession notice within 7 days of obtaining OC, with a date. Possession is given only on receipt of all instalments and dues.
  - cl. 7.1.7: the allottee takes possession within 15 days of the notified date.
  - cl. 7.1.8: fit-outs allowed after the notice, under fit-out guidelines and an interest-free fit-out deposit.
  - cl. 7.1.9: the allottee executes indemnities and undertakings at handover and is liable for maintenance whether or not possession is taken.
  - cl. 7.1.10: from 15 days after OC, the allottee pays a proportionate share (by carpet area) of outgoings: property taxes, water, insurance, common lights, staff salaries and so on.
  - cl. 7.1.11: the 8th/9th Schedule deposits are paid before possession; the unspent balance is handed to the society.
  - cl. 7.1.12: 5-year defect liability from the possession date in the notice.
  - — [Developer draft AFS](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1059_1766741592382-482534564.pdf)
- Under the AFS default clause, failing to take possession is an event of default with a 15-day cure period. — [Developer draft AFS cl. 9.1–9.2](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1059_1766741592382-482534564.pdf)
- Final instalment: the balance (5% in the Thane plan; the "balance amount" in the model) is due "on possession/receipt of OC/completion certificate". — [Developer allotment letter](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1359_1766816111271-620221494.pdf); [Open Exam Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/in-maharera-agent-coc/transaction-due-diligence-land-records/stamp-duty-registration-agreements)
- Delay interest continues until actual physical possession with OC conditions complied. — [LiveLaw (Bombay HC)](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/consumer-cases/bombay-high-court-upholds-maharashtra-real-estate-appellate-tribunals-decision-to-impose-interest-on-keyana-estate-for-delayed-possession-307568)
- MahaRERA orders in 2025 directed possession with OC within 30 days of the order, plus interest. A developer who took over a stalled project was held liable for delay interest until actual handover with OC. — [The Law Suits](https://thelawsuits.in/maharera-grants-relief-to-homebuyer-for-delay-in-possession/)

### Inferences
- Possession workflow: record the OC (per tower or wing; part OCs) with number, date and authority (CIDCO/NMMC/MCGM, etc.). Then:
  1. Auto-generate the final demand: balance consideration, GST, and the 8th/9th Schedule charges (society formation, share money, corpus, advance maintenance, utility deposits, legal charges, club fee, development charges).
  2. Issue the possession notice within 7 days of OC.
  3. Start maintenance billing from OC + 15 days.
  4. Run a no-dues check covering principal, interest, GST, TDS certificates, and the carpet-area true-up.
  5. Book a handover appointment and run a snag inspection checklist (room by room, with photos and status).
  6. Issue the possession letter with handover of keys, documents, warranties, manuals and meter numbers, plus the fit-out deposit.
  7. Collect a signed possession acknowledgement and indemnity.
  8. Start the defect-liability clock (5 years) and the complaint ticketing SLA (30 days to rectify).
- Track the s.19(10) two-month deadline so the promoter has evidence if the allottee delays.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 MahaRERA circular prescribing a standard possession letter or handover checklist was found. The S&R 2026 roundup on "execution of documents" circulars could not be read (parse error).
- No retrieved source defines statutory snag-list procedures in Maharashtra; these are practice, not law.

## 9. Society formation and conveyance; share money, corpus, maintenance before and after society formation

### Takeaway
Under RERA s.11(4)(e) and Maharashtra Rule 9, the promoter must apply to form the society within 3 months of 51% of units being booked. Conveyance deadlines (Rule 9(2)): for a single building, within 3 months of OC or of 51% of allottees paying in full, whichever is earlier. In a layout, a building's structure is conveyed within 1 month of society registration or 3 months of OC, whichever is earlier; the layout land goes to the apex body within 3 months of its registration or 3 months of the last building's OC. Under MOFA, conveyance is due within 4 months of society formation, and deemed conveyance is available if the promoter fails (Rule 9(4) allows a unilateral deemed conveyance under MOFA).

### Cited Findings
- Rule 9(2), the chart:
  - (ii) single building: conveyance within 3 months of OC or of 51% of allottees paying full consideration, whichever is earlier (if not otherwise agreed);
  - (iii)(a) building/wing in a layout: structure conveyed within 1 month of society registration or 3 months of OC, whichever is earlier;
  - (iii)(b) layout land: entire undivided land conveyed within 3 months of Apex Body/Federation registration or 3 months of the last building's OC, whichever is earlier.
  - Rule 9(3): the Authority can order conveyance. Rule 9(4): the society may get a unilateral deemed conveyance registered under MOFA.
  - MOFA s.11 with Rule 9: conveyance within 4 months of society formation.
  - RERA s.17(1): absent local law, within 3 months of OC.
  - GR of 22 June 2018 gives guidance for deemed conveyance across multi-building layouts.
  - — [IndiaLaw (13 Jul 2023)](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/civil/common-areas-division-under-conveyance-and-deemed-conveyance-of-societies/pdf/)
- Society within 3 months of 51% of total units being booked (s.11(4)(e) and Rule 9), secondary. — [Open Exam Prep: rights](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/in-maharera-agent-coc/allottee-rights-duties-delays/allottee-rights-protections)
- MOFA still operates. Deemed conveyance exists only under MOFA. — [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/mumbai/consumer-connect-mofa-still-exists-and-operational-says-expert)
- Association formation and conveyance timelines are part of the Order 38 non-negotiable list. — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/real-estate/1308918/mahrera-update-|-non-negotiable-clauses-in-the-agreement-for-sale)
- RERA s.19(9): each allottee must participate in forming the association/society/federation. — [CourtBook s.19](https://courtbook.in/real-estate-regulation-and-development-act-2016/section-19)
- Developer AFS for a large layout:
  - the promoter applies to form a co-operative society per tower under the MCS Act; the promoter may join for unsold units;
  - conveyance to the society within 3 months after all units in the tower are sold and paid for (a developer-specific trigger);
  - apex body application within 6 months of the last society conveyance;
  - apex conveyance within 6 months of apex registration;
  - society formation and conveyance costs (stamp duty, registration) are borne by the allottees or the society.
  - — [Developer draft AFS cl. 10](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1059_1766741592382-482534564.pdf)
- Payable before possession (developer AFS schedules):
  - 8th Schedule (non-refundable, no accounts to be rendered): society and apex formation and registration charges plus taxes; utility deposits at actuals; club membership fee plus taxes; legal costs plus taxes; development charges plus taxes.
  - 9th Schedule: share application money; corpus fund; proportionate share of taxes and levies; deposit towards provisional monthly contribution to outgoings. The unspent balance is transferred to the society.
  - — [Developer draft AFS](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1059_1766741592382-482534564.pdf)
- RWA maintenance GST exemption: ₹7,500 per month per member per apartment, and ₹20 lakh RWA turnover. — [CBIC Circular 109/28/2019-GST](https://cbic-gst.gov.in/pdf/circular-cgst-109n.pdf)
- Builder-managed maintenance before RWA formation is taxed differently (not detailed). A West Bengal AAR held sinking fund collections taxable as advances (persuasive only). — [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/gst-on-apartment-maintenance-what-residents-need-to-know-when-the-builder-is-in-charge)

### Inferences
- The CRM needs project-level triggers: % booked (alert at 51% plus a 3-month deadline for the society application), % fully paid (51% for the single-building conveyance trigger), OC dates per building, society registration date, apex body registration date, and computed conveyance deadlines with escalation.
- Society handover pack: member list with share certificates, the share money ledger, corpus and maintenance deposit accounts (collected, spent, balance), the audited maintenance account for the builder-managed period, utility deposits, and transfer of service contracts. Maintenance billing: builder-issued bills (with GST as applicable to the builder) from OC + 15 days until handover, then the society bills members. The CRM should support a cut-over date and transfer of balances.

### Gaps
- The Maharashtra Rule 9 gazette text was not retrieved directly. The 3-month/51% formation rule rests on secondary sources.
- The MOFA Rule 8 society formation timeline (4 months from majority booking) was not retrieved.
- Typical statutory share money values (for example ₹500 share + ₹100 entrance under MCS model bye-laws) were not verified.

## 10. Resale / transfer / nomination of a booking before possession

### Takeaway
Model-type agreements bar transfer or assignment of the booking or AFS until all dues are paid and without the promoter's prior written permission. Builders typically charge a transfer fee and some reserve a right of first refusal. The state said in 2012 that no developer NOC is needed for resale in a completed building, but for under-construction units a builder NOC or tripartite assignment is the norm. CIDCO transfer permission or charges may also apply on CIDCO leasehold land (until freehold conversion).

### Cited Findings
- The AFS bars letting, transfer, assignment, sale, or parting with any interest in the agreement or unit "until all the dues payable ... are fully paid and without the prior written permission of the Promoter". — [Developer draft AFS cl. 7.3](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1059_1766741592382-482534564.pdf)
- Allotment letter: the allottee may not transfer or assign the letter without the promoter's prior written consent. — [Developer allotment letter](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1359_1766816111271-620221494.pdf)
- A developer AFS ROFR (right of first refusal): the allottee sends an "Offer Letter" with the proposed transferee and price, and the promoter may buy at that price or waive. The sale must complete within 15 days of certain triggers or the process restarts. All stamp duty and registration on transfer documents is the allottee's. This is developer-specific, not part of the model. — [Developer draft AFS cl. 4.4.15, 17](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1059_1766741592382-482534564.pdf)
- 2012: no developer NOC needed for resale in fully constructed buildings. — [BCAJ](https://bcajonline.org/journal/no-need-for-developers-noc-for-flat-sale-transfer/)
- For under-construction resale, lawyers advise that a builder NOC and a tripartite agreement (seller, purchaser, builder) are needed, and builders "generally charge extra for cases of transfer which would be nominal" (forum opinions). — [CommonFloor forum](https://www.commonfloor.com/forum/T4facf324c9420-noc); [Kaanoon](https://www.kaanoon.com/87901/transfer-fees-charged-by-builder-in-mumbai)
- GST on an under-construction resale: views differ. One treats it as a transfer of the first buyer's rights, with the developer still liable for construction GST (forum, not authoritative). — [TaxTMI forum](https://www.taxtmi.com/forum/issue?id=119120)
- Builder "other charges" are taxed as independent services (MH AAAR). — [MH AAAR Puranik](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-02/mh_aaar_order_no._19-2022-23_of_puranik_builders_ltd.pdf)
- Nomination on death: the AFS lets the allottee nominate a person in the schedule. — [Developer draft AFS cl. 13](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1059_1766741592382-482534564.pdf)

### Inferences
- Transfer workflow:
  1. Request from the existing allottee.
  2. No-dues check (principal, interest, GST, TDS certificates).
  3. ROFR, if contractual.
  4. Transfer fee invoice (likely 18% GST; tax review).
  5. Bank NOC and loan closure, or a new lender's permission to mortgage.
  6. CIDCO transfer permission or charges for leasehold projects.
  7. Execution and registration of the deed of assignment or tripartite agreement (stamp duty on the transfer).
  8. KYC of the new allottee.
  9. Re-issue of the ledger. Receipts and TDS history stay with the original allottee: the old allottee's 26QB/16B relate to amounts they paid, and the new allottee deducts TDS only on future instalments.
  10. Update the RERA allottee list.
  - Keep a full audit trail of ownership changes.

### Gaps
- No authoritative source on Maharashtra stamp duty for assigning an under-construction agreement (whether set-off of duty already paid is allowed), on any MahaRERA cap on builder transfer fees, or on TDS treatment when a booking is assigned mid-way.

## 11. Cancellation and refund: forfeiture limits, refund timelines, interest, cancellation deed, GST/TDS on cancellation

### Takeaway
At allotment-letter stage, the MahaRERA model caps deduction on buyer cancellation at 0%, 1%, 1.5% or 2% of unit cost, depending on whether the request comes within 15, 16–30, 31–60 or 61+ days. The refund is due within 45 days without interest, and late refunds carry interest at SBI MCLR + 2% where the model provides for it. After the AFS, termination for buyer default needs three defaults plus notice, with a refund within 30 days. Buyer exit for promoter delay means a refund with interest from each payment date. On GST, the builder issues a credit note by 30 November following the end of the FY of supply. After that window, the builder refunds net of tax and the unregistered buyer claims the GST refund under Circular 188/20/2022-GST.

### Cited Findings
- Model allotment letter cl. 9 (buyer-initiated cancellation), deductions by when the letter is received:
  - within 15 days of the allotment letter: nil;
  - 16–30 days: 1% of the unit cost;
  - 31–60 days: 1.5%;
  - after 61 days: 2%.
  - The balance is refunded without interest within 45 days of the request.
  - — [Developer allotment letter](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1359_1766816111271-620221494.pdf); [Housing.com: MahaRERA directs Raymond to deduct 2%](https://housing.com/news/maharera-directs-raymond-to-deduct-2-on-flat-cancellation/)
- Promoters may increase the days or reduce the percentages, but not the reverse. Retaining the entire booking amount was held illegal (Raymond Realty order). — [Housing.com](https://housing.com/news/maharera-directs-raymond-to-deduct-2-on-flat-cancellation/); [WIRC-ICAI Dec 2023](https://wirc-icai.org/html/Newsletter/member/December-2023/maharera.html)
- Promoter-initiated cancellation at allotment stage (non-payment, or failure to register the AFS within 2 months): a 15-day notice, forfeiture of up to 2%, and the balance refunded within 45 days. If the refund is late, interest at SBI highest MCLR + 2%. — [Developer allotment letter cl. 3B, 12](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1359_1766816111271-620221494.pdf)
- After the AFS (Order 38 non-negotiable): termination for allottee default only after three defaults, written notice and email, and failure to cure. The refund, after final adjustment, is due within 30 days of termination. — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/real-estate/1308918/mahrera-update-|-non-negotiable-clauses-in-the-agreement-for-sale)
- The promoter has no statutory right to forfeit earnest money, and blanket forfeiture clauses have been struck down (secondary). — [Open Exam Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/in-maharera-agent-coc/transaction-due-diligence-land-records/stamp-duty-registration-agreements)
- Rule 19 (Maharashtra) requires refunds to be paid within 30 days of becoming due (secondary, unverified). — [Open Exam Prep: rules](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/in-maharera-agent-coc/maharera-rules-portal/maharera-rules-notifications)
- Promoter-default exit: the full principal plus interest from each payment date to the refund date (s.18(1)). — [CourtBook s.18](https://courtbook.in/real-estate-regulation-and-development-act-2016/section-18); [Open Exam Prep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/in-maharera-agent-coc/allottee-rights-duties-delays/delay-interest-compensation-math)
- GST credit note deadline: under s.34 as amended by s.102 of Finance Act 2022 (effective 1 Oct 2022), a credit note can be issued until 30 November following the end of the FY of supply, or the date of the annual return if earlier. — [BDO India digest](https://bdo.in/getmedia/d82272e9-bd66-4e69-8330-8d147b8d7b1e/BDO-India_Indirect-Tax-Weekly-Digest_06-October-2022.pdf.aspx?ext=.pdf); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/gst-proposals-union-budget-2022.html)
- CBIC Circular 188/20/2022-GST (27 Dec 2022): where the s.34 window has expired, the builder refunds the amount after deducting the tax it collected. The unregistered buyer gets a temporary registration with their PAN and files RFD-01 within 2 years. The relevant date is the date of the builder's cancellation letter. — [Circular 188/20/2022 (GST Council)](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-06/cir-188-20-2022-cgst.pdf); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/consumers-unregistered-persons-file-gst-refund-claim-cancelled-contracts.html)
- A credit note on cancellation with adjustment of tax paid is allowed if the amount is refunded to the buyer. — [GST FAQ Q22](https://www.gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2024-02/faq-real-estate-sector.pdf)

### Inferences
- Cancellation calculator inputs: stage (pre-AFS or post-AFS), initiator (buyer, promoter for buyer default, buyer for promoter delay), allotment letter date vs request date (for the 0/1/1.5/2% table), unit cost, receipts to date by head (principal, GST, stamp duty paid by buyer, other charges, TDS deducted).
- Outputs: forfeiture amount (capped); GST reversal via credit note if within the s.34 window, otherwise net-of-GST refund plus a buyer letter for the RFD-01 claim; interest payable where applicable (promoter delay: each receipt × 10.95% × days/365); refund-due date (45 or 30 days); a late-refund interest accrual; a cancellation deed or deed of cancellation registration tracker (stamp duty and registration paid on the AFS are typically not refundable by the builder; the buyer may seek a stamp duty refund from IGR, which is unverified here); and an unsold-inventory release.
- TDS on cancellation: the TDS the buyer deposited is credited to the builder's PAN, so the builder normally refunds that amount to the buyer as part of the refund. This is an inference; no source found.

### Gaps
- No primary source on how TDS already credited to the builder is handled on cancellation, or on Maharashtra stamp-duty refund (time limits under the Maharashtra Stamp Act for cancelled agreements). Rule 19's exact wording was not verified.

## 12. Receipts and accounting: receipt splits, cheque bounce, bank reconciliation, ERP/Tally integration, collection reports

### Takeaway
Since 1 July 2024 (MahaRERA Order No. 56, directions dated around 27 June 2024), projects must run three accounts in one scheduled bank: a Collection account (100% of receipts), a Separate RERA account (at least 70%, by auto-sweep, for land and construction costs) and a Transaction account (up to 30%). Withdrawals need Forms 1/2/3. Receipts must therefore be tagged to project and account. Model agreements allow the promoter to appropriate receipts across dues. Cheque dishonour follows NI Act s.138.

### Cited Findings
- MahaRERA Order No. 56 (effective 1 July 2024, for projects registered on or after that date): open three accounts in a single scheduled bank before applying for registration. They are the RERA Designated Collection Account (100%), the RERA Designated Separate Account (70%) and the RERA Designated Transaction Account (30%). Multiple-promoter projects may need a fourth or master account. — [Trilegal](https://trilegal.com/knowledge-repository/trilegal-update-maharera-issues-directions-for-maintaining-separate-bank-accounts-for-real-estate-projects/); [The Realty Today](https://therealtytoday.com/news/maharera-directs-real-estate-developers-to-maintain-three-bank-accounts-for-every-project-to-safeguard-buyers-interests); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/amp/mumbai/developers-cant-deposit-home-buyers-money-in-different-bank-accounts-maharera)
- The discussion paper (15 Mar 2024) said no withdrawals from the collection account, with auto-sweep of at least 70% to the separate account and up to 30% to the transaction account. The separate account is limited to land cost, construction cost, loan interest, and refunds or compensation to allottees. The basis is RERA s.4(2)(l)(D). — [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/consumer-cases/maharera-bank-account-real-estate-project-discussion-paper-252573); [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/maharera-proposes-3-bank-accounts-for-registered-projects/73741.html)
- MahaRERA froze 1,950+ project accounts at one point to protect homebuyers. Details and date are not confirmed in the retrieved result. — [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/maharera-bold-step-freezing-over-1950-real-estate-project-accounts-to-safeguard-home-buyers)
- Withdrawals need Forms 1/2/3 at each withdrawal (Circular 39/2021), and the annual audit is Form 5. — [WIRC-ICAI](https://old.wirc-icai.org/images/material/CH-28-CA-certificate-form3.pdf); [Free Press Journal](https://www.freepressjournal.in/mumbai/mumbai-maharera-ushers-in-era-of-transparency)
- AFS payment terms:
  - payments by account-payee cheque/DD/PO/RTGS/NEFT to the promoter account in the schedule (cl. 5.1.2);
  - lenders pay the same way (5.1.3);
  - the promoter may appropriate any receipt against any due, including interest, taxes, TDS shortfall (3.8, 5.1.9);
  - unpaid GST/TDS can be adjusted from later receipts with interest and penalty (5.1.5).
  - — [Developer draft AFS](https://resources.housiey.com/admin/uploads/project_legal_docs/1059_1766741592382-482534564.pdf)
- Cheque dishonour (s.138 NI Act), Supreme Court readings:
  - the cause of action arises only after a written demand and the drawer's failure to pay within 15 days of receiving the notice;
  - a complaint filed before the 15 days expire is invalid (Yogendra Pratap Singh v. Savitri Pandey, 2014);
  - the notice must specifically demand the cheque amount (Rahul Builders v. Arihant Fertilizers, 2007);
  - payment within the period ends s.138 liability.
  - — [Supreme Court judgment PDF](https://api.sci.gov.in/jonew/judis/17537.pdf); [Indian Kanoon](https://future.indiankanoon.org/doc/1388904)

### Inferences
- Receipt-split engine per receipt: allocate in a configurable order of appropriation (for example, interest, then taxes, then principal instalment, then other charges) or per the customer's instruction where the agreement does not allow appropriation. Store, for each receipt: principal portion by milestone, GST (CGST/SGST), TDS portion (expected vs certified), interest portion, other-charges portion (each with its own GST), payment mode, instrument number, bank, payer (customer or lender), and the RERA collection account it was credited to.
- Bank reconciliation: import bank statements for the collection, separate and transaction accounts. Match by virtual account number or UTR. Check that the 70/30 auto-sweep matches receipts. Flag unmatched credits as suspense.
- Cheque bounce: record the bank return memo date. Reverse the receipt and GST invoice adjustment. Levy bounce charges if contractual. Send the s.138 demand notice within the statutory period from the dishonour memo. Track a 15-day payment window from notice receipt, then a complaint deadline of one month after the cause of action. The 30-day notice period comes from s.138 proviso (b) as amended in 2002; that is background knowledge and the statute text was not retrieved.
- ERP/Tally integration: push customer ledgers, GST invoices, credit notes, receipts and TDS entries by project-wise GSTIN and RERA project ID. Collection reports: demand vs collection by milestone, ageing buckets, interest accrued, TDS pending certificates, loan disbursements pending, and RERA account balances vs the 70% rule.

### Gaps
- The full MahaRERA Order No. 56/2024 text (applicability to pre-July-2024 projects, multi-promoter rules, later amendments) was not retrieved.
- No authoritative source on Tally/ERP integration standards (implementation choice, not regulation).
- The NI Act s.138 statutory text (30-day notice window, s.142 limitation) was not retrieved directly; only court summaries.
